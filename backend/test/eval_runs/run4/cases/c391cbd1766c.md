## Query

Consider NSK's Confidentiality Agreement for Suppliers; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to share some Confidential Information with third parties, including consultants, agents, and professional advisors?

## Gold

Representatives: any employees, officers, directors, professional advisors or consultants in relation to each Party and any of its Affiliates, who are actively and directly engaged in the Purpose;
4.1. Notwithstanding clause 3.1 of this Agreement, the Receiving Party may disclose the Confidential Information:
a) to its and its Affiliate’s Representatives, provided always that the Receiving Party procures that such Representatives shall comply with the confidentiality obligations as set out in clause 3 of this Agreement, and the Receiving Party agrees to be liable for the actions or omissions of such Representatives in relation to the Confidential Information as if they were the actions or omissions of the Receiving Party; and

## RAG Answer

Yes, but only in a limited way.

Under NSK’s Confidentiality Agreement for Suppliers, the Receiving Party generally must not disclose Confidential Information to any person except as expressly permitted. However, Clause 4.1(a) provides an exception: the Receiving Party may disclose Confidential Information to its and its Affiliate’s “Representatives.”

“Representatives” are defined as employees, officers, directors, professional advisors, or consultants of a Party or its Affiliate who are actively and directly engaged in the Purpose. Therefore, sharing Confidential Information with consultants and professional advisors is permitted if they qualify as Representatives.

Any such disclosure is subject to two conditions:

1. The Receiving Party must ensure that the Representatives comply with the confidentiality obligations in Clause 3; and  
2. The Receiving Party remains liable for the Representatives’ acts or omissions regarding the Confidential Information.

The document does not expressly list “agents” as permitted Representatives. Therefore, disclosure to agents is not generally allowed unless the agent falls within the definition of “Representatives” or the disclosure is otherwise permitted, such as where required by a court or governmental body under Clause 4.1(b).
