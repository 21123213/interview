## Query

Consider the Non-Disclosure Agreement between NREL and Another Company; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to acquire information similar to the Confidential Information from a third party?

## Gold

d. The obligations of confidentiality set forth in this Agreement do not apply to information which (i) becomes publicly known without the fault of Receiving Party or DOE; (ii) has been made available by Disclosing Party (or the owner if other than Disclosing Party) to others without obligation concerning its confidentiality;

## RAG Answer

The Agreement does **not expressly allow** the Receiving Party to acquire information similar to the Confidential Information from a third party.

It only states that the confidentiality obligations do **not** apply to information that:

- becomes publicly known without fault of the Receiving Party or DOE;
- has been made available by the Disclosing Party to others without confidentiality obligations;
- is already in the Receiving Party’s or DOE’s possession without confidentiality obligations;
- is independently developed by employees of the Receiving Party or DOE without access to the Confidential Information; or
- is required to be disclosed by law or court order.

Therefore, if similar information is lawfully obtained from a third party and falls within one of these exceptions, it may not be subject to the Agreement’s confidentiality obligations. But the Agreement itself does **not** explicitly permit or address acquiring similar information from a third party.
