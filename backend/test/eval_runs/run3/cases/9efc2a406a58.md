## Query

Consider New York City Bar Association's Non-Disclosure Agreement; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to share some Confidential Information with their employees?

## RAG Answer

Yes. In the New York City Bar Association Model Form Non-Disclosure Agreement, the Receiving Party is referred to as the **Buyer**, and the confidential information is generally referred to as **Evaluation Material**.

Under **Section 2(a)**, the Buyer may disclose Evaluation Material to its **Representatives**, but only to the extent disclosure is needed to facilitate the evaluation, negotiation, and/or consummation of the Transaction.

“**Representatives**” is defined in **Section 1(e)** to include, among others, the Buyer’s **directors, officers, employees**, attorneys, accountants, consultants, and other advisors.

Therefore, the Buyer may share some Evaluation Material with its employees, provided that:

1. The employees are acting as Buyer’s Representatives;  
2. The disclosure is necessary to facilitate the Transaction; and  
3. Before giving access, the Buyer or one of its Representatives informs the employee of the Agreement’s terms and instructs them to comply with the applicable confidentiality obligations.

The Buyer is also generally liable for breaches by its Representatives, except where the Representative has signed a separate joinder agreement or a separate confidentiality agreement acceptable to the Company.
