## Query

Consider the Confidentiality Agreement between Tazza and CAFFE; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to share some Confidential Information with third parties, including consultants, agents, and professional advisors?

## Gold

Furthermore, the potential Franchisee agrees to not discuss or communicate any aspect whatsoever of the confidential information, be it directly or indirectly, to any agent, director, employee, or any other representative EXCEPT if these individuals (i) have been informed of the confidential nature of the information and (ii) have duly completed and signed the certificate presented in Annex “A”.

## RAG Answer

Yes, but only in a limited way.

Under the agreement, the Receiving Party (the potential Franchisee) generally **may not disclose Confidential Information to any third party** unless the Franchisor gives **written consent**.

However, the agreement contains an exception for certain people associated with the Receiving Party: it allows confidential information to be discussed or communicated to an **agent, director, employee, or any other representative** only if those individuals:

1. have been informed of the confidential nature of the information; and  
2. have completed and signed the certificate in **Annex “A”**.

The agreement also states that the Receiving Party will be accountable for violations by its **directors, agents, employees, representatives, and other professional representatives**, which suggests that professional representatives/advisors may fall within the permitted category if they meet the Annex “A” conditions.

That said, the document does **not expressly mention “consultants” or “professional advisors.”** Therefore, sharing Confidential Information with outside third-party consultants or advisors would generally require the Franchisor’s written consent unless they qualify as permitted representatives under the Annex “A” exception.
