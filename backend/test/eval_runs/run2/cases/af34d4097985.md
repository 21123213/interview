## Query

Consider New York City Bar Association's Non-Disclosure Agreement; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to share some Confidential Information with third parties, including consultants, agents, and professional advisors?

## Gold

(e) “Representatives” means, with respect to any Person, the Affiliates of such Person and any of its and their respective directors, officers, employees, managing members, general partners, attorneys, accountants, investment bankers, financial advisors, consultants and other advisors[, and any actual or potential sources of debt financing for such Person or its Affiliates]12; provided that, for the avoidance of doubt, in no event shall any actual or potential sources of equity financing for Buyer or its Affiliates be considered a “Representative” of Buyer for purposes of this Agreement without the prior written consent of the Company.
Buyer agrees that Buyer and its Representatives shall
(iii) disclose Evaluation Material only to Representatives of Buyer to whom disclosure is needed to facilitate the evaluation, negotiation and/or consummation of the Transaction.

## RAG Answer

Yes, but only in a limited, need-to-know manner.

The document uses the term **“Buyer”** as the receiving party and **“Evaluation Material”** as the confidential information. Under **Section 2(a)**, the Buyer may disclose Evaluation Material **only to its Representatives** if disclosure is needed to facilitate the evaluation, negotiation, or consummation of the Transaction.

The definition of **“Representatives”** in **Section 1(e)** includes:

- directors, officers, employees;
- attorneys, accountants, investment bankers, financial advisors, consultants, and other advisors;
- affiliates; and
- certain debt financing sources.

Therefore, sharing confidential information with **consultants and professional advisors** is permitted **if they are Representatives** and the disclosure is necessary for the Transaction. “Agents” are not expressly listed, but could qualify if they fall within the broad “Representatives” definition.

Before sharing, the Buyer must inform the Representative of the Agreement’s confidentiality terms and instruct them to comply. The Buyer is generally liable for breaches by its Representatives unless the Representative has a separate joinder agreement or confidentiality agreement with the Company.

The Agreement also permits limited disclosures outside the Transaction purpose in certain situations, such as **compulsory legal/regulatory disclosure** and disclosures necessary to **defend litigation**, subject to notice and confidentiality safeguards.

So, the short answer is: **Yes, the Buyer may share confidential information with certain third parties such as consultants and professional advisors, but only if they are permitted “Representatives” and only on a need-to-know basis for the Transaction.**
