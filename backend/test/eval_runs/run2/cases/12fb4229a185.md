## Query

Consider the Non-Disclosure Agreement between Dollar General and Portfolio Entity; Does the document indicate that the Agreement does not grant the Receiving Party any rights to the Confidential Information?

## Gold

As part of Buyer’s agreement to accept and use the Confidential Materials and Audit as such use is defined herein, and as consideration for the release of the Confidential Materials by Owner, BUYER HEREBY WAIVES ANY AND ALL ACTUAL OR POTENTIAL RIGHTS, CLAIMS, CAUSES OF ACTION OR DEMAND BUYER MIGHT HAVE REGARDING ANY FORM OF WARRANTY, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED, OF ANY KIND OR TYPE, RELATING TO THE CONFIDENTIAL MATERIALS.

## RAG Answer

No — not expressly.

Based on the provided NDA fragments, the document does **not contain an explicit clause stating that the Agreement grants the Receiving Party no rights to the Confidential Information**. Instead, it grants the Receiving Party — referred to as the **Buyer** — a **limited right to use** the Confidential Materials.

Relevant points:

- **Section 3** states that the Buyer and its Representatives “shall use the Confidential Materials solely for the purpose of evaluating a possible transaction with Owner.” This indicates a limited use right, not a complete denial of rights.
- **Section 3** also permits disclosure to Buyer’s Representatives who need to know the information for evaluating the transaction, subject to confidentiality.
- **Section 5** says the Confidential Materials are provided “solely as a courtesy” and that the Buyer waives rights/claims relating to warranties concerning the Confidential Materials. However, that is a waiver of warranty-related claims, not an express statement that the Buyer has no rights to the Confidential Information itself.

So, the Agreement indicates that the Buyer has only **limited, purpose-restricted use rights** and must keep the information confidential, but it does **not expressly state that no rights are granted** to the Confidential Information.
