## Query

Consider the Non-Disclosure Agreement between IGC and LSE; Does the document allow the Receiving Party to share some Confidential Information with third parties, including consultants, agents, and professional advisors?

## Gold

Representative means employees, agents, officers, advisers and other representatives of the Recipient.
The Recipient may disclose the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information to those of its Representatives who need to know this Confidential Information for the Purpose, provided that:

## RAG Answer

Yes, but only in a limited way.

Under the NDA, the Recipient generally must not disclose Confidential Information to any third party except as expressly permitted. The Agreement expressly permits the Recipient to disclose Confidential Information to its **Representatives** who **need to know** it for the Purpose.

“Representative” is defined to include **employees, agents, officers, advisers and other representatives**. Therefore, sharing with agents and professional advisers/consultants may be allowed if they qualify as Representatives and the need-to-know requirement is satisfied.

However, such disclosure is subject to conditions:

- the Recipient must inform the Representative of the confidential nature of the information;
- the Recipient must ensure the Representative complies with the NDA as if it were the Recipient;
- if requested by the Disclosing Party, the Representative must enter into a confidentiality agreement on equivalent terms; and
- the Recipient must keep a written record of the Representatives and remains liable for their non-compliance.

So, the document does allow limited sharing with certain third-party Representatives, such as agents and advisers/consultants, but only for the Purpose and subject to the above conditions.
